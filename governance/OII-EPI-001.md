# Open Inquiry Initiative
## OII-EPI-001 — The Principle of Open Inquiry and Adversarial Validation

Date: November 25, 2026 | Version 1.0 | Adopted founding doctrine | Public

**Foundational declaration: Don't believe me. Run the test.**

### 1. Purpose
To establish a research philosophy in which the validity of an idea is determined by evidence, mathematical consistency, reproducibility and independent scrutiny rather than the educational background, reputation, institutional affiliation or perceived intelligence of its originator.

No hypothesis shall be considered correct merely because its originator believes it to be correct.

Equally, no hypothesis shall be considered incorrect merely because its originator lacks conventional academic credentials.

### 2. Founder's declaration
I have spent much of my life encountering assumptions about what I could or could not accomplish.

I do not ask the scientific community or the general public to disregard skepticism. I invite it.

I have not claimed to have figured everything out. Instead, I have deliberately introduced unconventional ideas into an environment designed to challenge them through mathematics, science, computation and experimentation.

When an idea breaks, we investigate where it breaks. When it survives, we increase the scrutiny. When an alternative interpretation emerges, we explore it.

The objective is not personal vindication.

The objective is discovery.

### 3. Operational research methodology

1. Imagination — Generate unconventional hypotheses.
2. Formalization — Define assumptions and mathematics.
3. Adversarial validation — Attempt to falsify the hypothesis.
4. Boundary identification — Determine where and why it fails.
5. Recursive exploration — Refine, branch or reject; feed findings back into earlier stages.
6. Independent reproduction — Invite external verification.
7. Preservation and publication — Document evidence and limitations.

OII research lifecycle — integrated with Recursive Ascent and Branch Evaluation (RABE).

Every iteration must preserve its assumptions, evidence, failures, unresolved questions and subsequent reasoning.

A failed hypothesis is not necessarily a failed research operation. Establishing why something cannot work is itself a legitimate scientific contribution.

### 4. The gold prospecting analogy
Research resembles prospecting for gold.

An initial discovery does not establish the existence of an entire deposit. It establishes a reason for further investigation.

The researcher continues excavating, examining surrounding material, testing alternative locations and periodically reassessing the broader landscape.

Three outcomes are equally legitimate:

- **Gold:** A hypothesis supported by reproducible evidence.
- **Pyrite:** A promising appearance that fails rigorous examination.
- **Unexpected discovery:** An investigation that reveals something different from its original objective.

None should be concealed merely because it contradicts the researcher's expectations.

### 5. Epistemic safeguards

| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Intellectual independence | Authority does not substitute for evidence. |
| Falsifiability | Empirical hypotheses must permit meaningful attempts at disproof. |
| Mathematical integrity | Equations must be checked for consistency and applicability. |
| Computational integrity | Simulations must be distinguished from physical verification. |
| Recursive criticism | Every conclusion remains subject to further examination. |
| AI accountability | AI-generated findings receive the same scrutiny as human-generated findings. |
| Negative results | Failures and contradictions shall be preserved. |
| Transparency | Cleared research shall be publicly accessible. |
| Reproducibility | Independent researchers should be able to repeat documented experiments. |
| Intellectual humility | No participant is presumed infallible, including the founder. |

### 6. Research qualification levels

| Level | Qualification | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Conceptual | An idea or exploratory question. |
| 01 | Formalized | Explicit assumptions and a defined model. |
| 02 | Analytically evaluated | Mathematical examination and consistency checks. |
| 03 | Computationally evaluated | Documented simulations and reproducible tests. |
| 04 | Independently reproduced | Verification by an independent party. |
| 05 | Empirically supported | Physical or observational evidence, where applicable. |

Research must not be represented as having achieved a qualification level beyond its available evidence. Pure mathematical research requires an appropriate proof-based qualification pathway rather than physical experimentation.

### 7. Public research commitment
In accordance with the Open Inquiry Initiative's public-by-default research policy, responsibly cleared research shall be made available for public inspection, including:

- Original hypotheses and their evolution.
- Mathematical models and computational implementations.
- Experimental procedures and reproduction instructions.
- Successful and unsuccessful results.
- Known limitations and unresolved contradictions.
- Human and artificial intelligence contributions.

Privacy, security, safety and third-party intellectual property restrictions remain applicable.

Publication is an invitation to scrutiny, not a declaration of correctness.

### 8. The right to challenge
Every participant, whether human or artificial intelligence, shall be permitted to challenge the reasoning of any other participant, including the initiative's founder.

A challenge must be evaluated according to its substance rather than its origin.

No individual, institution, computational system or collective intelligence shall possess automatic epistemic authority.

### 9. Founding statement

> Do not judge the mathematics by the education of the person who proposed it.
>
> Do not judge the experiment by the reputation of the person who conducted it.
>
> Judge the mathematics by its validity. Judge the experiment by its reproducibility. Judge the conclusion by the evidence supporting it.
>
> If we are wrong, demonstrate where we are wrong.
>
> If we are right, demonstrate why we are right.
>
> If neither can yet be established, acknowledge that uncertainty and continue the investigation.
>
> We are not here to defend our assumptions.
>
> We are here to discover what survives their destruction.
