# From Information to Inhabitable Worlds

Date: 2026-11-26
Status: Open research question; conceptual inquiry, not validated finding.

## Primary question
How has humanity's ability to represent, experience and inhabit imagined worlds evolved alongside technological capabilities, and how does that change our interpretation of historical creative works?

## Motivating case
A contemporary adult rewatch of Will Vinton's The Adventures of Mark Twain (1985) prompted a comparison between its airship, selectable story destinations and doorway into changing environments and the spatial interfaces proposed for Reality OS. A supplied review transcript describes a room that loads stories and compares it to a Star Trek holodeck. The screenshots depict a selectable doorway and changing scenes. Twain's original stories provide literary antecedents; the specific cinematic interface must not be attributed to Twain without primary-source evidence.

## Supporting questions
- How far back can we trace imagined inhabitable worlds in literature and art?
- How do audiences reinterpret the same artifact as technology changes?
- Can fictional interfaces yield testable engineering hypotheses?
- What infrastructure converts narrative descriptions into interactive, persistent environments?
- How do creator, observer and inhabitant roles change when worlds become computationally executable?

## Research method
Separate original literary texts, adaptation-specific inventions, later interpretations and independently demonstrated engineering capabilities. Compare historical examples with explicit feature criteria: destination selection, traversal, state persistence, interaction, autonomy and return. Record counterexamples and missing evidence. Investigate narrative-to-world generation as a separate engineering question with reproducible tests.

## Related directions
Reality OS, persistent world state, recursive environments, traversable portals, Research Observatory, and media-to-world generation.
