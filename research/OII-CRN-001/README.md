# OII-CRN-001 — Civilizational Reset Narratives

Version 0.1.0 | 2026-11-27 | Preliminary, not independently validated

## Research question
How do mythology, literature and visual media represent civilization, catastrophic destruction, burial, renewal and loss of collective memory?

## Preliminary observations
The Mysterious Stranger sequence in Will Vinton's 1985 film The Adventures of Mark Twain depicts creation of a miniature inhabited world from clay, destructive intervention, lightning, collapse and disappearance of structures, and landscape renewal. These motifs invite comparison with modern civilizational-reset narratives, including mud flood claims. The comparison is interpretive, not evidence of historical events or intentional influence. Exact shot order and textual correspondence require verification against primary sources.

A further interpretive parallel concerns the power of creators over simulated worlds and the preservation or loss of their inhabitants' history. This is a philosophical analogy, not an empirical result.

## Competing explanations
- Biblical creation, flood and judgment imagery.
- Twain's philosophical exploration of suffering, morality and human insignificance.
- Adaptation-specific visual storytelling and clay animation techniques.
- Independent recurrence of catastrophe and renewal motifs.
- Direct influence between particular narratives, only if supported by dated documentary evidence.

No credible archaeological evidence establishes the recent global civilization-erasing catastrophe proposed by modern mud flood theory. Local floods, buried structures and changes in street levels do not independently establish a global reset.
