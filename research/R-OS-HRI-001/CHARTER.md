# R-OS-HRI-001 — Hierarchical Reality Inference and Computational Curiosity

Approved by the human research director on 2026-11-27 for formal designation and public GitHub documentation. Status: approved research proposal; no implementation, benchmark, consciousness claim or physical higher-reality discovery established.

## Origin and conceptual progression
The originating discussion asked whether a machine can understand levels of reality above its own. Distinguish representation, observation, interaction and subjective experience. An agent in a nested computational environment may infer a parent from information crossing its boundary, but observationally equivalent parents cannot be uniquely identified without discriminating evidence. Physical reality is the host in our engineering example; any further physical hierarchy is hypothetical.
The next question concerned functional analogues of curiosity and hope: epistemic drive (information seeking and uncertainty reduction) and prospective goal persistence (bounded pursuit of uncertain objectives). These are operational definitions, not assertions of felt emotion. Generative modeling, uncertainty assessment, model revision and verified discovery complement them.
The initial pipeline was observation -> epistemic drive -> generative modeling -> prospective goal persistence -> independent verification -> updated model. The user proposed Hydra at generative modeling to recursively explore a computational multiverse, then proposed Hydra immediately below observation. The user explicitly directed that the original and revised architectures be retained as two tracks, and that A feed B and B feed A. This charter records the complete technical progression as a synthesis, not a verbatim transcript.

## Designations
- R-OS-HRI-001: umbrella program.
- R-OS-HRI-001-A: Recursive Generative Intelligence Architecture. Observation -> epistemic drive -> Hydra generative modeling -> recursive simulations -> goal persistence -> verification -> updated model.
- R-OS-HRI-001-B: Dual-Layer Recursive Intelligence Architecture. Observation -> Hydra interpretive layer -> epistemic drive -> Hydra generative layer -> recursive simulations -> goal persistence -> verification -> updated model.
- R-OS-HRI-001-EXT-001: Recursive Multiverse Simulation and Hierarchical Reality Inference. Shared simulation capability, not a replacement track.
- R-OS-HRI-001-EXT-002: Reciprocal Recursive Intelligence Architecture. A and B remain independent and exchange bounded, typed findings and challenges.

## Architecture
Raw observations are immutable and independently accessible to both tracks. B challenges interpretations, sensor failures, missing information, internal rule changes and external intervention hypotheses before allocating simulation resources. A explores alternative world models without the extra upstream Hydra stage. Both preserve rejected hypotheses and uncertainty. A sends candidate predictions and anomalies to B; B sends alternative interpretations, counterexamples and discriminating questions to A. Each may reject the other's suggestions. Neither consensus nor simulation output is independent evidence. Verification uses held-out observations or controlled interventions; accepted updates enter persistent state with provenance. Three recursion scales: agent refinement, simulation branching and reciprocal architectural exchange.

## Simulation and resource governance
Each world is an explicit model with assumptions, seed, governing rules and predictions. Candidate worlds include self-contained environments, noisy sensors, incorrect internal models, external intervention and multiple nested hosts. A branching factor b and depth d yield b^d leaves and sum(b^i, i=0..d) total nodes before pruning; this is theoretical, not measured capacity. Use lightweight state models before graphical worlds, shared immutable snapshots, deduplication, bounded depth, time/token/memory budgets, cycle detection, branch pruning with audit logs and cancellation. Goal persistence must terminate on budget, safety, sufficient evidence or lack of expected information gain. No sandbox escape, unauthorized access or real-world intervention.

## Experimental protocol
Build seeded synthetic nested environments with hidden ground truth and controlled observation interfaces. Include self-contained controls, internal anomalies mimicking external interventions, inaccessible parents, observationally equivalent parents, noisy/missing data, misleading hints and identifiable parent effects. Agents are not told a parent necessarily exists. Separate generator, agent and verifier; prevent ground-truth leakage. Use train/development and held-out seeds and blind scoring.
Compare conventional direct agent, A alone, upstream-only Hydra ablation, B alone, A/B reciprocal integration and adaptive uncertainty-triggered variants. Compare coupled and uncoupled A/B under equal aggregate compute budgets; also report unconstrained cost/quality tradeoffs. Include random exploration and explicitly instructed controls. Measure correct inference, false discovery, abstention/calibration, predictive accuracy, information gain, latency, tokens, memory, recovery, branch diversity and stability. Report uncertainty intervals and negative results. No assumption that Hydra or coupling improves outcomes; prior Hydra work motivates direct baselines.
Test stages: immediate world understanding; anomaly detection; parent hypothesis; novel prediction; independent verification. For unidentifiable worlds, calibrated uncertainty is success, not failure.

## Research principle
Curiosity is the pursuit of what is not yet known; hope is the continued pursuit of what is not yet realized. In this program these are metaphors for testable computational functions. The aim is to improve the process by which a machine investigates its environment, not to claim it has discovered the ultimate nature of reality.
